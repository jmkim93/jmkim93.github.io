Universal Design Platform for an Extended Class of Photonic Dirac Cones

Jungmin Kim, Sunkyu Yu & Namkyoo Park

Physical Review Applied 13, 044015 (2020)

Publisher pageRead PDF

Instead of finding a different photonic crystal for every type of Dirac cone, this paper starts from one lattice and uses the ordering of its modes to design several dispersion classes.

Start with three modes, not a target shape

A square lattice of dielectric rods can support an accidental degeneracy between one monopole mode and two dipole modes at Γ. Changing their relative frequencies changes the nearby band crossings. This gives a more direct design handle than trying to reproduce the visual shape of a cone.

The spectrum tells the designer what to change

Near Γ, the three-mode description is

H(k)=(ωsvxkxvykyvxkxωpx0vyky0ωpy),H(k)u=ω(k)uH(\mathbf k)=\begin{pmatrix}\omega_s&v_xk_x&v_yk_y\\v_xk_x&\omega_{p_x}&0\\v_yk_y&0&\omega_{p_y}\end{pmatrix},\qquad H(\mathbf k)\mathbf u=\omega(\mathbf k)\mathbf uH(k)=​ωs​vx​kx​vy​ky​​vx​kx​ωpx​​0​vy​ky​0ωpy​​​​,H(k)u=ω(k)u

The diagonal entries are the monopole and dipole frequencies; the off-diagonal terms describe their wavevector-dependent coupling. Along ky = 0, the py mode decouples. Its position relative to the hybridized monopole–px branches determines the available crossings.

That model connects mode ordering to the local slope classes associated with type-I, type-II, and type-III dispersion. It is an effective description near Γ, not a substitute for calculating the full band structure.

Turn the modal target into a structure

The inverse-design procedure perturbs a target eigenmode and reconstructs the dielectric profile. This allows the dipole frequencies to shift relative to a retained monopole frequency within the same basic platform. Numerical designs then verify the different Dirac dispersions.

The study also examines how mirror-symmetry breaking changes a crossing into an anticrossing and how isofrequency contours evolve near the designed points. These results connect a tractable modal design rule to propagation behavior, while proposed non-Hermitian extensions remain future directions.
