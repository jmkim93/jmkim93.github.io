Research article

Control of localization and optical properties with deep-subwavelength engineered disorder

Seunghwa Oh, Jungmin Kim, Xianji Piao, Seulong Kim, Kihong Kim, Sunkyu Yu, Namkyoo Park

Optics Express 30, 28301-28311 (2022)

Publisher pageRead PDF

Layers far thinner than a wavelength might seem too small to matter individually. Near the critical angle, this study shows that their thickness disorder can strongly change optical transport.

A controlled test of very small disorder

The system is a multilayer film made from two alternating dielectrics. The study changes the individual layer thicknesses while preserving the total thickness and material fraction. That makes it possible to isolate the effect of structural arrangement rather than changing how much material the film contains.

A Metropolis-based method generates ensembles at prescribed values of an order metric, τ. This metric measures how the structure factor differs from an uncorrelated reference and is normalized against the periodic film. It turns “more or less disordered” into a controlled comparison.

Localization changes what passes through

The calculations examine transmission and localization near critical-angle illumination, where the response is sensitive to the multilayer arrangement. The numerical localization behavior agrees closely with the theoretical treatment of randomly stratified media.

In the cases studied, deeply subwavelength thickness disorder produces stronger transmission enhancement than the compared larger-scale perturbations. The individual layers therefore cannot always be replaced by an averaged medium when predicting the response in this regime.

The reflected beam shifts, too

Localized fields also contribute an additional lateral displacement of the reflected beam—the Goos–Hänchen shift. In the reported TE example, the additional shift reaches 11 times the conventional shift of the periodic reference; the study also finds enhancement for TM polarization.

That factor belongs to the specified structure and illumination conditions. The broader contribution is a method for connecting controlled structural statistics to transmission, localization, and beam displacement, making disorder a usable design variable.
