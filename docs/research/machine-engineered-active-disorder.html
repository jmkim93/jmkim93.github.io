Research article

Machine-Engineered Active Disorder for Digital Photonics

Jungmin Kim, Seungkyun Park, Sunkyu Yu & Namkyoo Park

Advanced Optical Materials 10, 2102642 (2022)

Publisher pageRead PDF

Two multilayers can look equally disordered yet respond very differently when an active material switches phase. This paper designs for that difference in response, not just for a chosen degree of structural order.

Make controllability the target

The platform is a 61-layer SiO₂/TiO₂ film with a central GST layer. Changing GST between amorphous and crystalline phases alters the angular transmittance. The question is how to choose the surrounding layer thicknesses so that the change has a desired form.

The paper quantifies the difference between the two angular responses using

This is the root-mean-square contrast over 90 sampled angles. A value of zero means the two GST phases give the same response; unity is the limiting case of complete transparent-to-opaque switching at every angle. The metric provides an optical design target that a structural order parameter alone does not supply.

Learn the forward problem, then design backward

A forward neural network predicts transmission from the layer thicknesses, and an inverse model searches for geometries that realize a target response. Numerical examples include angle-selective and broadband switching.

A modified model instead targets controllability directly. An auxiliary random seed allows it to generate multiple structures for the same target, reflecting the fact that an optical response does not uniquely determine a multilayer geometry.

A family of useful disorder

The generated families reveal that structures with similar conventional order metrics can have substantially different modulation responses. Within a chosen controllability class, other properties, including average transmittance and structural order, can be varied.

The contribution is both a design method and a way to classify active disorder by what it does. The evidence comes from learned models and numerical optical analysis of the multilayer platform; the paper does not report fabricated switching devices.
